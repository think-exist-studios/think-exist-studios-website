Crypto for beginners

From zero to your first wallet

A simple path for someone who has never purchased crypto before. Educational only — not a recommendation to buy a particular asset.

01

Choose where to buy

Use a reputable, regulated exchange or on-ramp that is available where you live. Create your own account, use a unique password, and turn on two-factor authentication before funding it.

02

Buy a small amount first

Start with an amount you can afford to lose while you learn. Review the total price, spread and fees before confirming. You do not need to buy a whole coin.

03

Decide where to hold it

Exchange custody: simplest for beginners, but the exchange controls the keys. Self-custody wallet: you control the keys and take responsibility for security. Hardware wallet: useful for larger or long-term holdings.

04

Match the network

Before withdrawing, confirm the receiving wallet supports the exact network you selected. Sending an asset on the wrong network or to an incompatible address can cause permanent loss.

05

Test before sending more

For a first transfer, send a small test amount. Confirm it arrives, then send the rest. Copy-and-paste the address and compare the beginning and end before you approve the transaction.

06

Keep records

Save transaction history, purchase prices, transfers and sales. Crypto activity can have tax consequences depending on your jurisdiction.

Wallet rules

Rules that protect your crypto

These habits matter more than chasing the next token.

Never share the recovery phrase

Your seed/recovery phrase and private key are master access. No legitimate support agent, moderator, website or airdrop needs them.

Back it up offline

Write the recovery phrase down and store it somewhere physically secure. Avoid screenshots, cloud notes, DMs and ordinary email.

Use a separate wallet for risky activity

Keep long-term holdings separate from the wallet you use for new dApps, mints, memes or experimental sites.

Verify the contract address

Token names and logos can be copied. Confirm the contract or mint address from the project's official sources before swapping.

Read what you sign

A wallet signature can authorize more than a simple login. Do not approve transactions or permissions you do not understand.

Never import a private key into a bot

Do not paste your seed phrase or private key into Telegram, Discord, a website form, a browser chat, or an unverified trading bot.

Beginner shortcut

The simple version

Buy through a reputable service → protect the account with 2FA → create a wallet only when you need self-custody or on-chain access → write the recovery phrase down offline → test transfers with a small amount → never share your seed phrase or private key.
